=== IN SHORT ===
EngProgress now sets one IELTS Reading passage a day that is the same for every visitor.
The passage takes about 20 minutes, is marked at once, and shows the sentence in the text that proves each answer.
Only a visitor's first attempt of the day counts, and it appears on the day's board under a generated nickname.
The board opens once five people have played that day, and a new passage arrives at midnight in Tashkent.
The challenge is free, needs no account, and does not use up the free daily practice.

=== BODY ===
Practising alone has one weakness. You finish a passage, you get 9 out of 13, and you have no idea whether that is good. Was the passage hard? Would other people have found it hard too?

From today there is a way to find out. Every day EngProgress sets **one Reading passage that is the same for everyone**. You read it, answer its questions, and see where your score sits among everybody else who took it that day.

## How it works

- **One passage a day.** It is an original passage in IELTS format with about 13 questions, the length of one section of the real test. Give it about 20 minutes.
- **The same for everyone.** Whoever opens the challenge today gets the passage you got. That is what makes the scores comparable.
- **Marked at once.** You see your score as soon as you finish, with the sentence in the passage that proves each answer and the reason a wrong option was wrong.
- **Your first score counts.** You can read the passage again afterwards, but only your first attempt goes on the board.
- **A new passage at midnight.** The day turns over at midnight in Tashkent.

You do not need an account, and it costs nothing.

## The board

When you finish, your score goes on the day's board, which is on the [front page](/#daily-challenge). The board lists the day's best scores. When two people have the same score, the one who finished earlier in the day is placed higher.

You appear under a nickname that the site makes for you, something like *Quick Falcon 42*. Nobody sees your name, because you never gave one.

:::tip Why the board is sometimes closed
The board opens once five people have taken the day's challenge. Before that you still get your score and your full marking, and the board fills in as others join. If you are early, you are one of the people opening it.
:::

## Why one passage a day is enough

Twenty focused minutes every day does more for Reading than three hours once a week. A single passage is short enough to finish properly and long enough to show a pattern. After a week of them, look at which questions you lose:

- If it is **True, False and Not Given**, the trouble is usually treating *the passage does not say* as *false*. [The question the passage never answers](/blog/true-false-not-given) explains the difference.
- If it is **matching headings**, you are probably matching words in place of ideas. There is [a five-step method](/blog/ielts-reading-matching-headings) for that.
- If you run out of time, practise with the clock visible, and stop at 20 minutes whether or not you have finished.

## How it sits beside the free daily practice

The challenge is separate from the free daily practice. You still have one free [Reading](/practice/reading), [Listening](/practice/listening) and [Writing](/practice/writing) practice every day, chosen for you, and taking the challenge does not use any of them up.

The difference is the point of each. The daily practice is yours alone: your own passage, at your own pace. The challenge is shared: one passage, the same day, everybody.

=== FAQ ===
Q: Is the daily Reading challenge free?
A: Yes. It is free and needs no account. You open the passage, answer the questions and see your marking straight away.

Q: Can I take the daily challenge more than once?
A: You can read and answer the passage again, but only your first attempt of the day counts for the board.

Q: Will my name appear on the board?
A: No. The board shows a nickname the site generates for you, such as Quick Falcon 42. The challenge does not ask for your name.

Q: When does the passage change?
A: At midnight in Tashkent, every day. Everyone gets the new passage at the same time.

Q: Does the challenge use my free daily practice?
A: No. The challenge is separate, so you still have your free daily Reading, Listening and Writing practice after taking it.
