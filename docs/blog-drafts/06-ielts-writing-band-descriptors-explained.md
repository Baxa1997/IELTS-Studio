=== IN SHORT ===
The IELTS Writing band descriptors mark every answer on four equally weighted criteria: Task Response or Task Achievement, Coherence and Cohesion, Lexical Resource, and Grammatical Range and Accuracy.
Task Response is often held back by a single fault, such as an essay with no clear position or one that answers only part of the question.
Coherence and Cohesion rewards logical paragraphs and accurate linking, and overused linking words can lower the band rather than raise it.
Lexical Resource and Grammatical Range and Accuracy both reward range and accuracy together, so ambitious language full of errors does not score higher than simpler language used well.

=== BODY ===
Every IELTS Writing answer is marked against the public IELTS Writing band descriptors, which describe nine bands for each of four criteria. The criteria are equally weighted, so each is a quarter of a task's mark. Knowing what each one rewards tells you where your next half band is hiding.

## Task Response and Task Achievement

In Task 2 this criterion is called **Task Response**. Did you answer every part of the question, take a clear position, and support it with relevant, developed ideas? In Task 1 it is called **Task Achievement**. In Academic Task 1, did you report the key features with an overview? In a General Training letter, did you cover every bullet point in a tone that suits the reader?

What usually holds it back:

- No clear position, or a position that changes halfway through the essay.
- Answering only part of a two-part question, such as *Why is this happening, and is it a positive development?*
- Ideas that are listed but never explained, supported or developed.
- Content that drifts away from the topic the question set.

:::tip Reread the question before the conclusion
Before you write your final paragraph, read the question again and check that you have answered every part of it. An excellent essay that answers half the question cannot score well for Task Response.
:::

## Coherence and Cohesion

This criterion asks whether a reader can follow you. **Coherence** is the logic: one main idea per paragraph, in a sensible order, each one developed before the next begins. **Cohesion** is the linking: words such as *however* and *as a result*, and reference words such as *this*, *these* and *such*, which tie sentences to each other.

The common trap is too much linking, not too little. Starting every sentence with *Firstly*, *Moreover*, *Furthermore* or *In addition* is what the descriptors call mechanical, and it does not score well. Link where the logic changes, and let pronouns and reference words do the rest.

## Lexical Resource

Lexical Resource is your vocabulary: its range, its precision and its accuracy. Higher bands use less common words and natural collocations, such as *a growing body of evidence* or *to address a problem*, with only occasional errors in spelling, word formation or word choice.

Two things pull it down: errors that get in the way of meaning, and words chosen because they sound impressive rather than because they fit. *The government should ameliorate the situation* is not better than *improve the situation* if you are not sure what *ameliorate* suggests.

## Grammatical Range and Accuracy

This criterion looks at the variety of your sentence structures and at how many of your sentences are free of errors. Range means a mix of simple and complex sentences: relative clauses, conditionals, passives, and clauses that begin with *although* or *while*. Accuracy means the share of sentences without errors, and punctuation counts here too.

The Band 7 description asks for a variety of complex structures and frequent error-free sentences. Range without control does not score well: a complex sentence with a broken structure costs more than a simple sentence written correctly.

## Where the next half band usually is

Because the four criteria count equally, your weakest criterion costs as much as your strongest one earns. If Task Response is at 6 and the other three are at 7, the quickest gain is almost always in Task Response, and the usual fix is a clearer position, stated in the introduction and kept to the end.

:::example One essay, two introductions
Unclear: There are many advantages and disadvantages of working from home, and people have different opinions about it.
Clear: Although working from home saves commuting time, I believe its costs to teamwork outweigh this benefit for most companies.
:::

The second introduction tells the examiner what the essay will argue, and every paragraph after it can be judged against that promise.

=== FAQ ===
Q: What are the four IELTS Writing criteria?
A: Task Response (called Task Achievement in Task 1), Coherence and Cohesion, Lexical Resource, and Grammatical Range and Accuracy. Each one counts for a quarter of a task's mark.

Q: What is the difference between Task Response and Task Achievement?
A: Task Response is the Task 2 criterion: answering every part of the question with a clear, supported position. Task Achievement is the Task 1 version: reporting the key features with an overview, or covering every bullet point of a letter in a suitable tone.

Q: Do more linking words improve the IELTS Coherence and Cohesion score?
A: No. Linking words at the start of every sentence count as mechanical cohesion and can lower the band. Use them where the logic changes, and use reference words such as this and these to connect ideas.

Q: Do spelling mistakes lower my IELTS Writing band?
A: Yes. Spelling is assessed under Lexical Resource. Occasional slips are compatible with high bands, but frequent errors, especially ones that make the meaning unclear, hold the band down.
