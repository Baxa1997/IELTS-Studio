=== IN SHORT ===
A good IELTS score is the one your university, employer or visa asks for, and most universities ask for an overall band between 6.0 and 7.0.
IELTS 7.0 to 8.0 lines up with CEFR C1, 5.5 to 6.5 with B2, and 8.5 or more with C2.
An overall 7.5 is a strong score that meets most university requirements, as long as every skill also clears the minimum the course sets.
IELTS recommends treating a result as valid for two years after the test.
Band 7 usually takes about 30 of 40 correct answers in Listening and Academic Reading, plus Writing and Speaking that stay clear and flexible under pressure.

=== BODY ===
What is a good IELTS score? Every candidate asks it, usually in a slightly different form: is 6.5 enough, is 7.5 good, how far is 7.0 from 6.5? The honest answer is that a good score is the one the reader of your certificate asks for. But the bands themselves mean something, and once you know what each one says about your English, the number on the results page stops being a mystery.

## What is a good IELTS score?

For most universities in English-speaking countries, a good IELTS score sits between **6.0 and 7.0 overall**. Undergraduate courses often ask for 6.0 or 6.5, many postgraduate courses for 6.5 or 7.0, and the most competitive programmes, such as medicine, law or journalism, for 7.0 or more. Professional bodies that register doctors and nurses often ask for around 7.0 in every skill. Visa rules are set by each country and change from time to time, so always read the current requirement from the official source.

![A student in headphones studying in a grand university library](/blog/what-is-a-good-ielts-score/library.jpg "Most universities ask for an overall band between 6.0 and 7.0. Photo: Zoshua Colah / Unsplash")

Most requirements have two parts: an overall band, and a minimum for each skill. A requirement written as *6.5 overall, with no skill below 6.0* turns away a candidate with 7.5, 7.0, 5.5 and 7.0, even though their overall band is 7.0.

:::tip Read the whole requirement
Before you book the test, write down both numbers: the overall band, and the lowest band allowed in any one skill. The second number is the one that most often decides the result.
:::

## What each IELTS band means

IELTS describes every band in a single line. These are the bands you are most likely to see, with the CEFR level each one lines up with:

:::example The IELTS band score chart
Band 9 · C2: Expert user — full operational command of English.
Band 8 · C1: Very good user — full command, with only occasional slips. IELTS treats it as borderline C2.
Band 7 · C1: Good user — operational command, with some inaccuracies in unfamiliar situations.
Band 6 · B2: Competent user — effective command despite some mistakes and misunderstandings.
Band 5 · B1: Modest user — partial command, coping with the overall meaning in most situations.
Band 4 · B1: Limited user — basic competence, limited to familiar situations.
:::

Half bands sit between the descriptions. A 6.5 is a candidate who is more than competent across the test, but not yet a good user in every skill.

## Is IELTS 7.5 a good score?

Yes. An overall **7.5** is a C1 score that meets the requirement of most universities, including many demanding postgraduate courses. It tells a reader that you handle complex language well and follow detailed reasoning, with only occasional slips. What can still trip you up is a single weak skill, so check that each of your four bands clears the course's minimum.

### Is 6.5 a good IELTS score?

For many courses, yes. IELTS 6.5 is a B2 score, and it is one of the most common requirements for undergraduate study and for many master's programmes. It is also the band where the next half band starts to need real work, because 7.0 asks for consistent control rather than occasional good moments.

### Is 6.0 enough?

For some courses and foundation programmes, it is. A 6.0 is a competent user: you communicate effectively, but mistakes and misunderstandings still show. If your course asks for more, many candidates find the step from 6.0 to 6.5 quicker than the step from 6.5 to 7.0.

## IELTS band scores and CEFR levels

Universities and employers increasingly ask for a CEFR level instead of a band. IELTS lines its bands up with the CEFR scale like this:

- **C2:** IELTS 8.5 to 9.0
- **C1:** IELTS 7.0 to 8.0
- **B2:** IELTS 5.5 to 6.5
- **B1:** IELTS 4.0 to 5.0

Treat the mapping as a guide rather than a conversion. IELTS itself notes that Band 8 sits on the border of C2, and that many 6.5 candidates already work at C1, so a requirement that must be sure of C1 will usually ask for 7.0. For what each level means in practice, read [CEFR levels explained](/blog/cefr-levels-explained).

## How long is an IELTS score valid?

IELTS recommends that results are treated as valid for **two years** after the test. Some organisations accept older results, and some want a score taken more recently, so check with the one you are applying to before you book. Time the test so that the result is still inside the window on the day your application is assessed, not only on the day you send it.

![A plane climbing through the clouds at sunset](/blog/what-is-a-good-ielts-score/plane.jpg "Plan the test date backwards from your application deadline. Photo: Andrea De Santis / Unsplash")

If one skill lets you down, you may not need to sit the whole test again. [One Skill Retake](/blog/one-skill-retake) explains when resitting a single skill makes sense.

## How the overall band is worked out

The overall band is arithmetic: add your four skill bands, divide by four, and round to the nearest half band. An exact quarter rounds up, so an average of 6.25 becomes 6.5 and an average of 6.75 becomes 7.0.

:::example Two candidates, half a band apart
Candidate A: Listening 7.0, Reading 7.0, Writing 6.0, Speaking 6.5. The average is 6.625, so the overall band is 6.5.
Candidate B: Listening 7.0, Reading 7.0, Writing 6.5, Speaking 6.5. The average is 6.75, so the overall band is 7.0.
:::

Half a band in Writing was the whole difference between them. ## How to get Band 7 in IELTS

Band 7 is where many plans end, because it opens most university doors. It asks for something different in each skill.

![A hand writing notes at a wooden desk beside a cup of coffee](/blog/what-is-a-good-ielts-score/writing.jpg "Task 2 counts twice as much as Task 1 in the Writing band. Photo: Unseen Studio / Unsplash")

- **Listening:** about 30 correct answers out of 40. Most lost marks are spelling, plurals and answers written in the wrong form, so check every word you write against the instructions.
- **Reading:** about 30 out of 40 in Academic Reading, and about 35 in General Training. Keep to roughly 20 minutes a passage, and learn how [True, False and Not Given](/blog/true-false-not-given) questions really work.
- **Writing:** a clear position held all the way through, ideas that progress from one paragraph to the next, less common vocabulary used with few errors, and a mix of complex sentences, many of them free of errors. Task 2 counts twice as much as Task 1, so give it 40 of the 60 minutes.
- **Speaking:** talk at length without visible effort, use vocabulary flexibly across a range of topics, and mix simple and complex structures. In Part 2, [a one-minute plan](/blog/speaking-part-2-one-minute-plan) keeps the long turn going.

:::tip Start with your weakest skill
Because the overall band is an average, the cheapest half band is usually in your weakest skill. Find it before you plan your study weeks, and spend the first of them there.
:::

=== FAQ ===
Q: What is a good IELTS score?
A: The one your university, employer or visa asks for. Most universities ask for an overall band between 6.0 and 7.0, and competitive courses often ask for 7.0 or more, usually with a minimum band for each skill.

Q: Is IELTS 7.5 a good score?
A: Yes. An overall 7.5 is a C1 score that meets the requirement of most universities, including many demanding postgraduate courses, as long as each skill also clears the course's minimum.

Q: What CEFR level is IELTS 6.5?
A: IELTS 6.5 is usually B2, at the top of the 5.5 to 6.5 range. IELTS notes that many 6.5 candidates already work at C1, but a requirement that must be sure of C1 will usually ask for 7.0.

Q: How long is an IELTS score valid?
A: IELTS recommends treating results as valid for two years after the test. Some organisations accept older results, so check with the one you are applying to.

Q: How many correct answers do I need for Band 7?
A: About 30 out of 40 in Listening and in Academic Reading, and about 35 in General Training Reading. The exact number changes slightly between versions of the test.

Q: Is IELTS 6.0 enough for university?
A: For some undergraduate and foundation courses, yes. Many degree courses ask for 6.5, and many postgraduate courses for 6.5 or 7.0, so check the requirement of the exact course you are applying to.
