=== BODY ===
Almost every IELTS app now promises the same thing: write an essay, get a band and some feedback. That is the price of entry, not a reason to choose one. So it is fair to ask what EngProgress actually does that the others do not — and whether it is worth paying for.

## A band you can trust

The most expensive mistake an IELTS app can make is telling you that you are ready when you are not. A generous 7.0 feels good for a week, and then the real result arrives. You lose the exam fee, the weeks of waiting, and sometimes a university deadline.

Our grader is built the other way round. It reads your work criterion by criterion, quotes the evidence from your own text, and when you sit between two bands it **rounds down** and tells you exactly what is missing for the higher one. Hearing "more work needed" is annoying; a false 7.0 is far worse.

## One essay, many drafts

Most apps score an essay and move on. You get a number, you write a new essay, and the same mistake comes back. EngProgress keeps you on the same essay: you fix what capped your band, resubmit, and see whether the fix worked. That revision loop is how a teacher works, and it is the part of the product we care about most.

## Speaking that talks back

Speaking is where most platforms stop at a recording button. EngProgress gives you three ways to practise out loud.

- **The full three-part mock.** An AI examiner runs Part 1, the Part 2 long turn and the Part 3 discussion in real time, follows up on what you actually said, and gives you a band at the end.
- **Part 2 practice.** Cue cards with the one-minute preparation and the two-minute long turn, so you learn to fill the time.
- **The speaking tutor.** Not a test but a lesson. You talk, the tutor reacts to your words, corrects one thing at a time by quoting what you said, and shows you a better English sentence.

## A tutor that speaks your language

Beginners often know what they want to say but cannot yet say it in English. The speaking tutor lets you choose the language it **explains** in: English only, Uzbek, Russian, or automatic, where it follows the language you switch to. You can change it in the middle of a lesson, the moment you realise you need help.

The practice language never changes. Whatever you choose, you speak English, and the screen shows only the recommended English sentence — the explanation is there to get you to it, not to replace it.

The tutor also adapts to why you are learning: general English, everyday situations, a presentation for work, a job interview, or IELTS coaching.

:::tip Use the support language as a ladder
Start in Uzbek or Russian if English feels too hard, then switch to English only as soon as you can follow. The less you lean on translation, the faster your speaking grows.
:::
